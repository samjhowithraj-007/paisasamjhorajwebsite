What is a SIP?

A SIP (systematic investment plan) invests a fixed amount into a mutual fund on a set date every month. It is a way of investing, not a type of fund. Because the amount is fixed, you buy more units when prices are low and fewer when they are high. This is called rupee-cost averaging.

Common questions

How much should I start a SIP with?

Start with an amount you can keep paying even in a bad month. Being regular matters more than the size. Many funds accept SIPs from ₹100 to ₹500 a month; check the scheme page.

Is SIP return guaranteed?

No. A SIP in an equity fund moves with the market and can show a loss for months or a year or two. The calculator uses an assumed return only to show how compounding works.

What is a step-up SIP?

A step-up SIP raises the monthly amount every year, usually with your salary increase. Even a 10% yearly step-up makes a big difference over 15 to 20 years.
