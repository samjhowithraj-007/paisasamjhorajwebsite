Money Cheat Sheet

Khushiyan poori, kharcha control mein. One page for the whole festival · Paisa Samjho Raj

1. Fix the Diwali cap first

Before any shopping, write ONE number for the whole festival: gifts, sweets, clothes, lights, pooja, travel. Pay from this cap, never from the Future (savings) jar.

2. Split the cap (example: ₹20,000)

BucketShare₹
Gifts + sweets35%7,000
Clothes25%5,000
Home, lights, pooja20%4,000
Travel10%2,000
Buffer10%2,000

3. Next Diwali, no shock

Diwali comes on a date we already know. Spread it over the year:

This year's cost ÷ 12 = save every month

₹24,000 ÷ 12 = ₹2,000 a month in a separate "Diwali jar" from next month.

4. Sale check before you tap "Buy"

  • Need, want or just pressure? (Ask honestly.)
  • Big item? Sleep on it for 24 hours.
  • Compare the final price with delivery, fees and taxes, not the "MRP cut".
  • Is it inside the cap? If not, something else goes out.

5. "No-cost EMI" check

  • Is there a processing fee?
  • Is GST charged on the interest part?
  • Do you lose a cash or card discount by choosing EMI?
  • Add it all up: total paid vs the cash price.

6. Credit card: pay the full bill

"Minimum due" only stops the late fee. Interest runs on everything you leave unpaid, at up to about 3.75% a month (45% a year). Shopping on the card? Keep that amount aside the same day.

7. Festival fraud: 5 rules

  • You never enter your UPI PIN to receive money. A "collect request" asks you to pay.
  • "You won a Diwali gift / lucky draw" by SMS or WhatsApp: delete it. Don't click the link.
  • Never share an OTP, PIN or password, even with someone who says they are from the bank or the police.
  • Check the website address before paying; big discounts on fake sites are the bait.
  • Lost money? Call 1930 (national cyber fraud helpline) at once, tell your bank and report on cybercrime.gov.in.

8. Five-minute check after Diwali

Write down what you really spent in each bucket. Over the cap? Note where, and set next year's monthly Diwali jar (step 3) today. Chhota-chhota daalo, bada-bada banao!
